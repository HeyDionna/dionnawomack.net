Conversation design

Elara, a D&D sidekick

Dungeon Masters juggle rules, NPCs and story notes mid-session. Elara answers fast and feels like part of the world: a persona with her own art and moods, intent matching for the questions DMs actually ask, three reply variants per intent, and fallbacks that point you somewhere useful.

What I learned: the fallback line gets read more than any other, so it deserves the most care.

Built for a real trip

Ho Ho Ahoy, a cruise companion

I'm taking a Christmas cruise with a group that ranges from about 22 to 60, and we needed real headcounts, plus-ones included, before we sail. So I built the group an app. It installs from the browser, works on sea days with no signal, and syncs RSVPs to a Google Sheet through Apps Script once someone's back online.

Inside: a boarding-pass home screen with a countdown, the day-by-day itinerary, RSVPs and headcounts for group plans, a packing list that grows with what you sign up for, and games (bingo, a scavenger hunt, trivia and a predictions reveal).

Design call: the organizer is a user too, so plans, reminders, bingo prompts and hunt prompts all live in the spreadsheet. Updating the app never takes code.
